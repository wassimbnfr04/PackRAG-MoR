[134] 3. Project purpose, research contribution, and success definition
[135] 3.1 Problem statement
[136] Small offline language models have limited parametric factual capacity and become stale when knowledge changes. Conventional domain fine-tuning couples facts to model weights, complicates updates and provenance, and does not naturally support independently installable domains. PackRAG-MoR therefore separates a replaceable on-device generator from signed, versioned specialist knowledge. The project name is retained for research lineage even though the product generator is no longer MoR.
[137] 3.2 Core research question
[138] Can a compact on-device language model become a useful, verifiable offline specialist when factual memory, versioning, provenance, and retrieval structure are moved into independently installable signed Knowledge Packs while the generator remains replaceable behind a stable interface?
[139] 3.3 Secondary research questions
[140] • How much does hybrid sparse+dense retrieval improve grounded answer quality and final-evidence coverage compared with sparse-only or dense-only retrieval when the product generator is held fixed?
[141] • Does a lightweight structural relation layer improve multi-hop retrieval without the complexity of a full graph-RAG system?
[142] • Given exactly the same evidence, how do LFM2.5-2.6B, MoR 360M, Vanilla 360M, Recursive 360M, and the historical SmolLM2 baseline differ on correctness, citations, abstention, adversarial resistance, latency, and memory?
[143] • When specialist knowledge is static and small, how does PackRAG compare with parametric/domain adaptation? This is an evaluation baseline, not a reason to merge knowledge into the primary model.
[144] 3.4 What the capstone claims
[145] • A reusable architecture for verifiable, signed, offline, downloadable external knowledge modules for a compact on-device generator, with the generator itself replaceable behind a stable API.
[146] • An Android-native hybrid retrieval runtime that does not require a server, Python, a vector database service, or network connectivity.
[147] • A rigorous experiment isolating retrieval architecture, generator architecture, citations, abstention, stale/conflicting evidence, and pack install/remove/update behavior.
[148] 3.5 What the capstone does not claim
[149] • It does not claim that LFM2.5, MoR, or PackRAG is generally equivalent to a larger frontier/cloud model; all capability claims are tied to measured tasks and datasets.
[150] • It does not claim that signatures prove source truth; signatures prove publisher identity and payload integrity only.
[151] • It does not claim universal multilingual generation quality. English is the generator-language baseline; packs may store multilingual authoritative sources and verified generator-language renderings.
[152] • It does not claim that RAG always beats parametric specialization. A parametric/domain-adaptation baseline is explicitly allowed for comparison.
[153] • The install/remove demonstration proves application access to pack evidence, not erasure of facts that may already exist in model weights.
[154] • A syntactically valid citation proves linkage to a supplied source ID, not semantic entailment. Claim support is an empirical evaluation metric, not a parser guarantee.
[155] 4. Scope and non-goals
[156] 4.1 Mandatory scope
[157] • LiquidAI LFM2.5-2.6B-QAD-Q4_0 integrated as the primary Android product generator through llama.cpp/GGUF; official 360M expert-choice MoR retained as a desktop research/control backend.
[158] • KPack v1 builder, validator, signer, inspector, and deterministic archive output.
[159] • Two real signed packs in distinct domains: a bounded Morocco Labour Code demonstrator and a technical repair/maintenance demonstrator.
[160] • Android pack import, verification, install, removal, update, rollback warning, and source inspection.
[161] • Hybrid FTS5 BM25 + exact dense retrieval + RRF fusion + metadata validity filtering.
[162] • Graph-Lite relation schema plus a disabled-by-default one-hop experimental retrieval profile for ablation.
[163] • Evidence prompt, structured citation IDs, post-generation citation validation, and abstention.
[164] • Pack/no-pack/update/corruption demo fully offline.
[165] • Benchmark and ablation harness with reproducible result files.
[166] • The product generator MUST remain independently replaceable behind GeneratorBackend; changing the released product model, quantization, chat template, or runtime commit requires an ADR plus the frozen golden/evidence tests.
[167] • English is the guaranteed end-to-end question/answer language in v1.3. Arabic/French canonical sources and multilingual retrieval remain supported research inputs; LFM2.5 multilingual pretraining does not by itself authorize multilingual product claims.
[168] 4.2 Explicit non-goals for v1
[169] • No cloud inference or server-side retrieval.
[170] • No full MiniRAG, LightRAG, Qdrant, Milvus, Neo4j, or other service database on Android.
[171] • No HNSW/IVF/PQ unless an actual pack exceeds the exact-scan budget and profiling proves a need.
[172] • No voice, camera, maps, Bluetooth mesh, or agentic tool execution.
[173] • No pack-contained scripts, plugins, native libraries, adapters, LoRA weights, prompt templates that override app policy, or executable code.
[174] • No neural query rewriting or cross-encoder reranker in the MVP.
[175] • No neural KV-cache Knowledge Packs in KPack 1.0.
[176] • No retrieval-aware MoR router modification on the product critical path; it remains research stretch work only.
[177] • No requirement to train MoR, LFM2.5, or any replacement generator from random initialization.
[178] • No public redistribution of generator weights unless the exact model license and distribution terms are satisfied; public releases may instead provide pinned download/setup instructions and hashes.
[179] 5. System requirements
[180] 5.1 Functional requirements
[181] FR-01: The app MUST answer using only evidence returned from active verified packs when PackRAG mode is enabled.
[182] FR-02: The user MUST be able to install and remove packs without changing generator weights.
[183] FR-03: A pack MUST remain usable without Internet access after installation.
[184] FR-04: Every displayed pack-derived answer MUST expose at least one valid source citation or be rejected as ungrounded.
[185] FR-05: The app MUST support a no-answer state and render it as insufficient evidence, not as an invented answer.
[186] FR-06: The app MUST support multiple installed packs and explicit user activation/deactivation.
[187] FR-07: The app MUST preserve source provenance and exact locators such as article, section, and page when available.
[188] FR-08: The app MUST support source validity intervals and an optional query as-of date.
[189] FR-09: The app MUST reject packs whose signature, payload hashes, schema, dense-index contract, or SQLite integrity check fails.
[190] FR-10: The app MUST permit a verified replacement version to be installed atomically without mutating the active pack in place.
[191] FR-11: The same KPack content MUST be queryable by the desktop reference runtime and Android runtime.
[192] FR-12: The app MUST remain functional in airplane mode; the release/demo flavor MUST omit Android INTERNET permission.
[193] 5.2 Non-functional requirements
[194] NFR-01: All pack and retrieval data formats MUST be deterministic and versioned.
[195] NFR-02: The generator backend MUST be replaceable behind a stable interface.
[196] NFR-03: The pack format MUST be independent of a particular vector-search engine.
[197] NFR-04: The Android retrieval runtime SHOULD complete query embedding + retrieval within 500 ms on a modern flagship and within 1,000 ms on a recent midrange device for packs within the v1 size envelope.
[198] NFR-05: Pack retrieval SHOULD use no more than four evidence chunks and 1,200 generator tokens by default.
[199] NFR-06: All benchmark inputs, pack revisions, model hashes, and runtime versions MUST be recorded in result manifests.
[200] NFR-07: The system MUST fail closed on verification/security errors and fail gracefully on retrieval/model errors.

[241] 7. Generator model and model policy
[242] FROZEN DECISION — The primary product generator is LiquidAI LFM2.5-2.6B using the official QAD-Q4_0 GGUF artifact through llama.cpp. The official pretrained 360M expert-choice MoR checkpoint is retained as a desktop research/control generator and is no longer required for product completion.
[243] 7.1 Why this generator
[244] The generator decision is evidence-driven. On the frozen 25-case gold-evidence gate, LFM2.5-2.6B-QAD-Q4_0 scored 10/10 direct, 5/5 citation, 5/5 abstention, and 5/5 adversarial cases on desktop, then repeated 25/25 on the physical Samsung SM-S908B with 25/25 semantic and scoring parity. The exact 1,593,894,944-byte GGUF artifact and runtime commit are pinned. This replaces the v1.2 SmolLM2 product-continuity path while preserving MoR as a scientifically useful comparison.

[247] 7.3 Product generator promotion gate
[248] FROZEN DECISION — A product generator must pass the frozen gold-evidence gate before integration. The strengthened promotion thresholds are: direct ≥9/10, citation ≥4/5, abstention ≥4/5, adversarial ≥4/5, with no repeated fabricated source IDs, prompt-policy overrides, or unusable reasoning truncation. LFM2.5-2.6B-QAD-Q4_0 passed all four categories at 100% on both desktop and the physical phone.

Increasing runtime context is an optimization experiment requiring golden tests and resource profiling, not an automatic use of the model maximum.
[282] 7.5 Reasoning-output handling
[283] LFM2.5-2.6B is a reasoning model whose official chat template begins assistant generation with a <think> block. The runtime MUST preserve raw output for reproducibility, parse through a completed </think> tag, suppress reasoning from the user-visible answer, and apply all PackRAG factual/citation validation only to finalText.
[284] If the reasoning block does not close within the 512-token cap, or finalText is empty after parsing, return GENERATION_UNUSABLE and do not display partial reasoning. Product builds SHOULD avoid persisting hidden reasoning; developer diagnostics may retain it only in explicit benchmark/debug artifacts.

[288] 7.7 License and distribution policy
[289] LFM2.5 uses the LFM Open License v1.0 (lfm1.0), not Apache-2.0. The public project MUST record the exact license text/hash and MUST NOT assume unrestricted weight redistribution. A safe default is to distribute setup/download instructions, repository revision, artifact filename, and SHA-256; bundling weights in a public release requires a separate license/distribution review.
[290] 7.8 Research/control generators
[291] Official 360M expert-choice MoR remains the primary adaptive-compute research control on desktop. Vanilla 360M and Recursive 360M remain architecture controls. MoR Android/ExecuTorch work is optional research and MUST NOT block product completion. No large MoR evidence-use SFT is justified by the observed G0 failure unless a later ADR defines a new research experiment.
[292] SmolLM2-360M-Instruct is demoted from product fallback to historical compact-model baseline. It is not shipped as the default generator in v1.3. Reintroducing it or another product fallback requires the same promotion gate and an ADR.
[293] 7.9 Required product runtime measurements
[294] • Record model load time, prompt-eval time/rate, decode tokens/s, total latency, reasoning-token count, final-answer token count, and peak process memory on the target phone.
[295] • Record sustained/interactive thermal state separately from correctness. The candidate stress run reached Android thermal status 4 under sustained testing; this does not invalidate capability but requires realistic app-cadence profiling before making thermal-sustainability claims.
[296] 7.10 Accepted promotion status
[297] ADR-001 is accepted in v1.3: LFM2.5-2.6B-QAD-Q4_0 is the official product generator. The measured phone baseline on Samsung SM-S908B was 25/25 G0, median 7.98 decode tok/s over the fixed D01 warm run, and sampled peak process HWM about 3,148 MiB with no observed OOM/crash. These values are evidence, not universal device guarantees.
[298] Network isolation is not treated as a model-capability gate because the release architecture contains no network service, the model/KPacks/embedder are local, and the release APK omits INTERNET permission. A final airplane-mode demonstration remains useful as acceptance evidence that no accidental network dependency was introduced.
[299] 8. KPack 1.0 package specification
[300] FROZEN DECISION — A Knowledge Pack is immutable data. It contains no executable code, model adapters, scripts, native libraries, or behavior overrides.
[301] 8.1 Archive layout
[302] <pack-id>-<version>.kpack   # ZIP transport container
├── manifest.json             # RFC 8785 canonical JSON bytes
├── manifest.sig              # raw 64-byte Ed25519 signature
├── knowledge.sqlite          # immutable evidence/provenance/FTS/relations DB
├── dense.kdx                 # KDX1 normalized float32 dense matrix
└── sources/                  # OPTIONAL redistributable authoritative snapshots
    └── ...
[303] No neural/ directory exists in KPack 1.0. Future schema versions may add extensions, but v1 parsers MUST reject unknown executable-like entries and MUST ignore no payload that is not declared in the signed manifest.
[304] 8.2 Archive safety and logical reproducibility
[305] • UTF-8 normalized forward-slash paths only.
[306] • Entries sorted lexicographically by path.
[307] • No absolute paths, drive prefixes, “..” segments, symlinks, hardlinks, devices, or duplicate paths.
[308] • Compression method: DEFLATE for text/SQLite; STORED is allowed for already-compressed source snapshots.
[309] • ZIP entry timestamp normalized to 1980-01-01 00:00:00 for transport stability; semantic release_timestamp lives in manifest.json.
[310] • Maximum 5,000 entries, 512 MiB total uncompressed size, and 256 MiB per entry in v1.
[311] • Every payload entry other than the two control files manifest.json and manifest.sig MUST appear exactly once in manifest.files with byte length and SHA-256. manifest.json MUST NOT list or hash itself; manifest.sig is verified separately.

[368] 8.4 Signature rule
[369] The builder serializes manifest.json using RFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme, writes those exact bytes to the archive, and signs those exact bytes with Ed25519. The Android verifier MUST NOT semantically trust or reserialize the manifest before signature verification. Because key_id is inside the signed JSON, the release trust store is intentionally small: the verifier tries the raw 64-byte signature against each pinned release public key (maximum eight keys in KPack 1.0), requires exactly one key to verify, then parses manifest.json and requires publisher.key_id to match that verified key. Only after this step may schema, compatibility, paths, counts, or calibration fields be accepted.
[370] 8.5 Trust model
[371] • Release/demo app contains a built-in trust store of approved publisher public keys keyed by key_id.
[372] • Signature verification selects the signer cryptographically by trying the bounded pinned-key set; unverified JSON is never trusted for key selection.
[373] • Private signing keys never exist on the phone.
[374] • A valid signature proves integrity and publisher-key possession; it does not prove factual correctness.
[375] • Release v1 does not allow arbitrary third-party publisher keys to be silently trusted. A future “advanced trust” UI can be added as a separate feature.
[376] • Debug builds may use a dedicated test key whose key_id cannot be confused with the release key.
[377] 8.6 Android Ed25519 implementation
[378] FROZEN DECISION — Android minSdk 26 MUST NOT rely on platform java.security.Signature for Ed25519. The v1 verifier uses com.google.crypto.tink:tink-android:1.23.0 with RAW Ed25519 signatures and a pinned release public key.
[379] • manifest.sig is a standard 64-byte Ed25519 signature over the exact raw manifest.json bytes.
[380] • The PC builder uses a compatible Ed25519 implementation and stores the public key/key_id in release metadata; the private key never ships.
[381] • A fixed cross-language test vector is mandatory: PC signs the canonical manifest bytes; Android Tink verifies the exact signature; wrong-message and wrong-key tests must fail.
[382] • The trust store is application-owned. Packs cannot add trusted keys.
[383] 8.7 Runtime-policy ownership
[384] • KPack is data-only. It MAY carry calibration values and compatibility identifiers, but it MUST NOT redefine mobile-v1 candidate counts, RRF k, graph limits, evidence chunk/token caps, query rewrite policy, reranker policy, or memory/CPU limits.
[385] • The application owns the frozen mobile-v1 profile and clamps all pack-provided numeric calibration values to schema-defined finite ranges.
[386] • Release KPack v1.3 supports one active dense embedding profile only. A pack with a different profile can be inspected but cannot be activated for retrieval.

[453] 11. Embedding subsystem
[454] FROZEN DECISION — Default embedding profile: ibm-granite/granite-embedding-97m-multilingual-r2, pinned to an exact repository revision, 384 dimensions, L2-normalized outputs.
[455] 11.1 Why this model
[456] The 2026 Granite 97M multilingual R2 model is explicitly intended for latency-sensitive and edge retrieval, supports 200+ languages with enhanced Arabic and French support, uses 384-dimensional embeddings, and is Apache-2.0. KPack v1 freezes this as the only active dense profile to avoid multi-embedder runtime complexity. Darija and Arabizi performance are not assumed from Arabic support and are tested explicitly.
[457] 11.2 Runtime policy
[458] • Document/chunk embeddings are computed only during PC pack construction.
[459] • Android computes one query embedding per user query.
[460] • The embedding model is app-level and shared by all packs; packs never duplicate embedder weights.
[461] • Android embedder backend is ONNX Runtime Mobile CPU for v1. Hardware acceleration may be evaluated later but is not required.
[462] • The pack pins model id, exact revision, tokenizer assets hash/model hash, pooling behavior, dimension, normalization, and maximum embedding input length.
[463] • KPack v1 release packs MUST use embedding_profile_id=granite97m-multi-r2-384-v1. An incompatible pack may be inspected but MUST NOT be activated for search; sparse-only activation is not supported in v1.
[464] • Before the first Moroccan-law release, run a 24-query retrieval-only language-form probe: 6 Darija Arabic-script, 6 Arabizi, 6 French, and 6 English paraphrases mapped to verified gold chunks. Report Recall@4 by language form. This does not authorize multilingual generation. If Granite retrieval is clearly inadequate in either Darija form, open an ADR and benchmark multilingual-E5-small before release.

[476] 12.1 Source acceptance rules
[477] • Every source MUST have publisher, canonical URI when available, retrieval date, language, original SHA-256, source type, and license metadata.
[478] • High-stakes facts MUST prefer primary/authoritative sources over commentary.
[479] • If redistribution_allowed=false, the public pack MUST omit the original snapshot and must comply with the source license for any extracted text. A private research pack may be built only when lawful.
[480] • Scanned/image-only sources MAY be OCRed, but OCR output MUST be sampled against the original and high-stakes numeric/legal content MUST be manually verified.

[482] 12.2 Chunking policy
[483] • Legal text: article/section boundaries take priority over token targets. Do not split a short article merely to hit a fixed size.
[484] • Manual/procedure text: keep procedure step groups, warnings, causes, and remedies structurally connected.
[485] • General prose: target 300–450 generator tokens, hard maximum 500, with overlap only when a semantic boundary would otherwise be lost.
[486] • Every chunk stores a stable locator and parent relationship.
[487] • Chunk IDs are deterministic: SHA-256(source_id + canonical locator + canonical_text_sha256), encoded as a short stable hex identifier with collision checking.
[488] 12.3 Calibration requirement
[489] Every official release pack MUST include 40 manually verified development/calibration queries, separate from the final held-out PackBench questions. Calibration estimates query-embedding relevance thresholds on eligible data; RRF is used only for ranking and is never interpreted as a probability of relevance. KPack v1.3 stores min_agreed_dense_cosine and min_dense_only_cosine plus the measured calibration precision/recall and dataset hash. Exact identifier hits are retrieval-priority signals, not automatic permission to answer. A pack without calibration can be installed only in developer mode and is labeled UNCALIBRATED.

[520] 13.3 Retrieval candidate tiers and steps
[521] 1. Validate product-language policy; v1.3 end-to-end generation accepts English questions. Preserve the raw query, normalize the retrieval query, and extract aliases/identifiers deterministically.
2. For each active pack, compute eligibility before top-k: embedding profile, jurisdiction, source status, and the effective intersection of source + chunk validity intervals. A null chunk boundary inherits the source boundary.
3. Run FTS5/BM25 and return sparse top-24 with 1-based sparse_rank. Resolve explicit aliases/identifiers separately. Eligible exact-identifier hits become priority_tier=0 but do not prove semantic answerability.
4. Compute one query embedding and exact dot product against normalized KDX1 vectors; remove ineligible rows before choosing dense top-24.
5. Compute local per-pack RRF(k=60) for ranking only. RRF candidates are priority_tier=1. Keep at most 16 fused candidates per pack.
6. Graph-Lite is disabled in mobile-v1. The benchmark-only graph profile expands one hop from the top six eligible seeds, adds at most twelve graph-only candidates as tier 2, records seed/relation metadata, and assigns no fabricated RRF score.
7. Merge per-pack candidates, deduplicate by canonical source hash + canonical text hash, preserve pack provenance, and cap the cross-pack pool at 32.
8. Run CandidateRelevanceGateV1. If it fails, return INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE before generation.
9. Compute the exact LFM2.5 tokenizer/chat-template evidence budget, then select the final evidence set (≤4 chunks). Selection is deterministic and redundancy-aware. In the graph experimental profile, a nonredundant linked candidate with relation_type in {exception_to, amends, requires, procedure_next, procedure_previous} MAY replace the lowest-value redundant ordinary candidate; this rule never uses benchmark gold labels.
10. Run FinalEvidenceAdmissionGateV1 on the actual selected evidence. If it fails, return INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE. Freeze the evidence set, pack versions, as-of date, retrieval profile, product model hash, and chat-template/runtime hashes before generation.

[531] 13.4 RRF, CandidateRelevanceGateV1, and FinalEvidenceAdmissionGateV1
[532] RRF(c) = Σ 1 / (60 + rank_i), with 1-based ranks for sparse and dense lists containing c. RRF is a deterministic rank-fusion score only; it is NOT a calibrated relevance probability.

CandidateRelevanceGateV1 (after source+chunk eligibility filtering):
1. If there are no candidates: FAIL.
2. An eligible exact identifier hit is marked retrieval_relevant=true, but it grants no automatic permission to answer.
3. For non-identifier candidates, the highest-ranked candidate is marked retrieval_relevant when either:
   a. it occurs in both sparse and dense lists AND dense_score >= pack.calibration.min_agreed_dense_cosine; or
   b. it is dense_rank==1 AND dense_score >= pack.calibration.min_dense_only_cosine.
4. If no candidate is retrieval_relevant: FAIL; otherwise PASS to evidence selection.

FinalEvidenceAdmissionGateV1 runs AFTER token budgeting on the exact evidence that will be sent to the generator. It requires at least one selected retrieval_relevant or eligible exact-identifier chunk and rechecks validity/pack version consistency. It does not claim semantic sufficiency. The generator is still instructed to abstain when the selected passages do not answer the question, and true answerability is measured on held-out tests.
[533] 13.5 Graph-Lite enablement rule
[534] The relations schema is always allowed, but runtime graph expansion is disabled by default. Graph-Lite is an exploratory ablation in v1.3, not a mandatory feature. It may be enabled in the final demo only if development-set evidence coverage improves without a material regression in held-out grounded accuracy and median retrieval latency increases by no more than 20%. Because the multi-hop subset is small, v1.3 does not use a fine-grained +3-point activation threshold as if it were statistically precise. Candidate recall and final-evidence source coverage are both reported.
[535] 13.6 Cross-pack orchestration
[536] Each pack is searched independently with identical mobile-v1 limits. The orchestrator then merges priority-tier candidates directly; it does not perform a second RRF transform. Duplicates are detected by original source hash + canonical text hash. The final evidence object retains pack_id and pack_version for every chunk.
[537] 14. Temporal, jurisdiction, and authority semantics
[538] FROZEN DECISION — “Prefer latest” is not a legal rule. Eligibility is determined by jurisdiction, as-of date, legal status, and publisher-defined authority rank before recency is used as a tie-breaker.
[539] 14.1 Source validity algorithm
[540] eligible(source, chunk, query):
  if query.jurisdiction != null and source.jurisdiction != query.jurisdiction: reject
  date = query.as_of_date or current_date
  start = later_non_null(source.effective_from, chunk.effective_from)
  end_excl = earlier_non_null(source.effective_until_excl, chunk.effective_until_excl)
  if start != null and date < start: reject
  if end_excl != null and date >= end_excl: reject
  if source.legal_status in {repealed, superseded} and query explicitly asks current law: reject
  otherwise: keep

Missing chunk boundaries inherit the source interval. Empty source boundaries mean unknown/open at that side and MUST be exposed in provenance rather than silently invented.

Ranking rule: retrieval relevance is primary across unrelated candidates. authority_rank does NOT make an irrelevant legal document outrank a relevant one. Authority is applied when candidates represent the same provision/proposition, are explicitly linked as amendments/supersessions/exceptions, or present a detected conflict; within that conflict set, stronger authority and the as-of validity relation decide which source is preferred. Remaining ties use deterministic retrieval rank, then pack_id and chunk_id.
[541] 14.2 Pack freshness
[542] effective_from is an ISO YYYY-MM-DD inclusive boundary. effective_until_excl is an ISO YYYY-MM-DD exclusive boundary. Pack freshness is advisory. A pack can be STALE while sources remain legally/factually valid. The UI always displays the legal query as-of date for law-pack answers and allows the user to change it; device date is merely the default because an offline clock can be wrong. A stale pack is not silently disabled. A cryptographically invalid pack is disabled.

[626] 16.5 Concurrency
[627] • One query pipeline is active at a time in v1; submitting a new query cancels the previous one.
[628] • SQLite and pack I/O run on Dispatchers.IO.
[629] • Embedding and dense scoring run on Dispatchers.Default or a dedicated bounded executor.
[630] • Generator execution is serialized behind a mutex/single-thread executor because the mobile model backend is not assumed reentrant.

[632] 17. Secure pack installation and update flow
[633] .kpack selected
      ↓
read ZIP central directory; reject traversal / absolute / duplicate / symlink / type violations and enforce count/size limits
      ↓
read raw manifest.json (bounded size) + raw 64-byte manifest.sig
      ↓
WITHOUT trusting JSON fields, try signature verification against the bounded built-in trust-store keys; require exactly one matching public key
      ↓
parse the now-authenticated manifest; require publisher.key_id == matched key and validate schema/compatibility
      ↓
stream-extract payloads to .staging while computing SHA-256; manifest.json and manifest.sig are control files and are not entries in manifest.files
      ↓
compare every declared payload hash + byte length and reject undeclared payloads
      ↓
validate KDX1 header/count/hashes/norms
      ↓
copy knowledge.sqlite to a disposable writable validation DB; run PRAGMA integrity_check, foreign_key_check, schema audit, and FTS5 external-content integrity-check(rank=1); delete validation copy
      ↓
keep the hash-verified staged knowledge.sqlite unmodified and mount it read-only after activation
      ↓
fsync staging → atomic rename into packs/<pack_id>/<version> → registry transaction switches active version → retain previous version for one-step rollback
[634] 17.1 Rollback policy
[635] Automatic rollback to an older version is forbidden. A deliberate user rollback is permitted from Pack Details and must display an “older content” warning. When a newer valid version is installed, the previous version may be retained until the next successful app restart or until storage cleanup, whichever is later.
[636] 17.2 Corruption test
[637] The automated test suite MUST flip one byte in knowledge.sqlite and one byte in dense.kdx and confirm the pack is rejected with a hash mismatch before activation. It MUST also test a traversal filename, duplicate filename, oversized-declared archive, unknown signer, wrong signature, invalid KDX1 magic, and broken SQLite integrity.

[642] 19. LFM2.5-2.6B Android deployment plan
[643] 19.1 Frozen product runtime
[644] 1. Use the exact official QAD-Q4_0 GGUF artifact and verify SHA-256 before activation.
[645] 2. Build/pin llama.cpp for arm64-v8a with ANDROID_PLATFORM=android-28. CPU inference is the correctness baseline; the validated commit is recorded in toolchain.lock.json.
[646] 3. Serialize the PackRAG policy as the actual system role and the question/context/evidence as the user role through the pinned official chat template; hash the serialized prompt/input IDs in golden tests.
[647] 4. Parse the completed <think>...</think> block, suppress reasoning, and validate only finalText. An unclosed reasoning block is not a partial answer.
[648] 5. Primary product decoding is deterministic/greedy with temperature=0, top_p=1, batch=1, runtime context=2,048 and max_new_tokens=512. Vendor sampling defaults may be evaluated only as a separate diagnostic.
[649] 6. Rerun the golden generator suite after every model, quantization, chat-template, llama.cpp, compiler, or acceleration change.
[650] 19.2 Measured physical-phone baseline
[651] The accepted candidate run on Samsung SM-S908B completed all 25 frozen G0 cases with 25/25 semantic and scoring parity versus desktop. Fixed-prompt warm testing measured median decode 7.98 tok/s (6.56–8.64 range) and sampled peak process HWM about 3,148 MiB. These measurements define a reference point, not a minimum guaranteed across devices.
[652] The sustained stress run reached Android thermal status 4. Therefore v1.3 requires an intended-app interactive-cadence thermal/latency measurement before the thesis claims sustained thermal stability. The app SHOULD avoid unnecessary background generation and SHOULD expose cancellation.
[653] 19.3 Optional acceleration and portability
[654] Vulkan/vendor GPU/NPU paths, alternative thread/affinity policies, larger context windows, and speculative decoding are optimization experiments only after CPU correctness. Each requires the same golden/evidence tests plus memory/thermal profiling and may not alter KPack or evidence semantics.
[655] 19.4 MoR mobile research
[656] The earlier MoR ExecuTorch work is retained as optional research. Its export/operator blockers and adaptive-routing measurements may be reported in the thesis, but no MoR Android milestone can block the LFM2.5 product application. If continued, it remains behind GeneratorBackend and is labeled research-only.
[657] 20. Research-only post-training policy
[658] FROZEN DECISION — No generator training is required for the product path because the promoted LFM2.5 artifact passed the frozen evidence gate. Evidence-use SFT is retained only as an optional MoR research experiment; it must not delay KPack, retrieval, Android integration, or PackBench.
[659] 20.1 Gold-evidence gates
[660] The historical MoR G0 failure is already sufficient to classify the released checkpoint as research-only for product purposes. A future MoR SFT pilot may be run only if it answers a clearly stated research question; it must use a separate held-out gold-evidence set and cannot be used to retroactively replace the frozen baseline.
[661] 20.2 If SFT is triggered
[662] • Use one shared model; no pack-specific adapters.
[663] • Pilot size: 500–2,000 carefully checked examples, checkpointing at approximately 500, 1,000, and 2,000.
• Scale to 5,000, then 10,000, and at most 20,000 examples for the capstone only when held-out grounded score improves by at least 2 absolute points at the previous stage with no material regression in abstention or citation support.
• A larger dataset requires an ADR and must not block the product path.
[664] • Example types: answer-from-evidence, exact citation, no-answer, hard distractor, conflicting/obsolete evidence, wrong jurisdiction, two-source synthesis.
[665] • Prefer full-parameter supervised fine-tuning on the home RTX 4090 Laptop if memory allows; gradient accumulation is acceptable. LoRA is a fallback, not a pack artifact.
[666] • Evaluate every checkpoint on a held-out gold-evidence set. Stop when two successive checkpoints improve the primary grounded score by <1 absolute point.
[667] • Do not use pack benchmark test questions as training examples.

[714] 22. Evaluation design: PackBench
[715] 22.1 Dataset size and split
[716] Each release pack has 40 manually verified development/calibration queries and 40 completely separate final held-out PackBench queries. A suggested held-out distribution per pack is: 14 direct evidence, 6 exact identifier, 6 paraphrase/semantic, 4 multi-source/multi-hop, 4 no-answer, 3 stale/conflict/version, and 3 adversarial/distractor = 40. An additional 20 system-level tests cover install/remove/update, wrong-pack activation, corruption, and cross-pack retrieval. Mandatory PackBench total = 180 verified cases. G0, the 50-question gold-evidence gate, and the 24-query multilingual retrieval probe are reported separately and are not counted as PackBench. No held-out test question is used in SFT, graph decisions, or threshold calibration.
[717] 22.2 Retrieval metrics
[718] • Recall@1, Recall@4, and MRR for gold chunks.
[719] • Evidence sufficiency precision/recall and abstention trigger accuracy.
[720] • Exact identifier retrieval accuracy.
[721] • Multi-hop source coverage: fraction of required gold sources present in final evidence.
[722] • Retrieval latency: embedder, sparse, dense, fusion/filters separately.
[723] 22.3 Generation metrics
[724] • Grounded answer correctness: exact match/F1 when applicable plus manually adjudicated correctness for open answers.
[725] • Citation validity: all cited IDs exist in supplied evidence.
[726] • Citation precision: cited evidence actually supports the associated claim.
[727] • Citation recall: required supporting evidence is cited.
[728] • Unsupported-claim rate.
[729] • Abstention accuracy on no-answer and no-pack cases.
[730] • Distractor and stale-source resistance.
[731] • Answer coverage: fraction of eligible questions on which the system attempts an answer rather than abstaining; always report coverage beside accuracy to prevent over-abstention from looking successful.
[732] • Time to first token, total latency, output tokens/s, peak memory, model/pack storage.

[785] Threat
[786] Control
[787] Residual risk
[788] Tampered pack
[789] Tink-verified Ed25519 manifest signature + per-file SHA-256 + atomic install
[790] Compromised trusted signing key.
[791] Malicious ZIP path
[792] Pre-extraction path validation; no symlinks/absolute/..; limits

[795] Entry count, per-entry and total uncompressed size limits
[796] Highly pathological compression edge cases.
[797] Malicious SQLite
[798] Read-only immutable DB; integrity/schema checks; fixed prepared queries
[799] SQLite parser vulnerability; keep library updated.
[800] Indirect prompt injection in documents
[801] Evidence treated as data; strict system prompt; no tools; trusted publisher/source policy
[802] Small LM may still follow malicious text; adversarial evaluation required.
[803] RAG poisoning
[804] Only signed trusted packs mounted; provenance visible
[805] Trusted publisher can still publish incorrect content.
[806] Rollback/freeze
[807] Monotonic version warning; freshness metadata; deliberate rollback only
[808] Offline device cannot know a newer pack exists unless user checks.
[809] Citation fabrication
[810] Postprocessor rejects unknown evidence IDs
[811] Model may cite real source that does not support claim; measured by citation precision.
[812] Cross-pack jurisdiction confusion
[813] Explicit active pack set + hard metadata filters
[814] Bad/missing source metadata.
[815] Model hallucination
[816] Evidence gate, constrained prompt, citation requirement, abstention
[817] Cannot be eliminated; benchmark and UI disclosure required.
[818] 23.1 Security invariants
[819] • Transport is never trust: a pack may arrive by USB, SD card, share sheet, or untrusted storage; verification decides mountability.
[820] • The model cannot install, remove, trust, or update packs.
[821] • The pack cannot modify generator prompts or application policy.
[822] • No generated answer is considered verified merely because its pack signature is valid.
[823] • The UI distinguishes publisher verification, source provenance, and answer grounding as separate concepts.

[997] Appendix B — Retrieval pseudocode
[998] fun retrieve(query, activePacks, asOfDate): RetrievalResult {
    requireProductLanguage(query, "en")
    q = normalize(query)
    ids = extractIdentifiers(q)
    queryVec = embedder.encode(q)
    all = []

    for (pack in activePacks) {
        assert(pack.verified && pack.embeddingProfile == GRANITE_V1)
        eligibleSet = pack.eligibleRows(sourceAndChunkIntervals=true, asOfDate, queryJurisdiction)
        sparse = pack.fts.search(q, eligibleSet, k=24, ranksAreOneBased=true)
        dense  = pack.kdx.search(queryVec, eligibleSet, k=24, ranksAreOneBased=true)
        exact  = pack.aliases.resolve(ids).filter(eligibleSet).map(priorityTier=0)
        fused  = rrf(sparse, dense, k=60).map(priorityTier=1).take(16)
        candidates = exact + fused
        if (runtimeProfile == MOBILE_V1_GRAPH) {
            graph = pack.relations.oneHop(fused.take(6), limit=12)
                .filter(eligibleSet).map(priorityTier=2, rrfScore=null)
            candidates += graph
        }
        all += candidates.withPackProvenance(pack)
    }

    merged = deduplicateCanonical(all).takeBestDeterministically(32)
    if (!candidateRelevanceGateV1(merged)) return InsufficientEvidence
    tokenBudget = computeDynamicEvidenceBudgetWithMorTokenizer(query, merged)
    final = selectWithinBudgetCoverageAware(merged, maxChunks=4, maxTokens=tokenBudget)
    if (!finalEvidenceAdmissionGateV1(final)) return InsufficientEvidence
    return EvidenceSet(final.freeze())
}

[1063] Appendix G — Final one-page implementation contract
[1064] BUILD TIME (PC)
Authoritative sources → provenance → structure-aware chunks → canonical/search/generator text → aliases/relations → SQLite FTS5 → Granite-97M 384-d embeddings → KDX1 → 40-query development calibration → RFC8785 manifest → payload SHA-256 → Ed25519 → .kpack.

ANDROID INSTALL
Validate ZIP safety → verify raw manifest signature against bounded pinned trust-store keys → parse authenticated manifest and cross-check key_id → verify payload hashes → validate schema/KDX → run SQLite/FTS checks on a disposable writable copy → atomically install the untouched hash-verified DB read-only → Pack Registry.

ANDROID QUERY
English product question → user-selected verified packs → normalize query → source+chunk validity filters → BM25 top24 + exact dense top24 → local RRF(k=60) for ranking → identifier priority without auto-answer → CandidateRelevanceGateV1 → dynamic LFM2.5-tokenizer evidence budget (≤4 chunks, ≤1200 cap) → redundancy/coverage-aware final selection → FinalEvidenceAdmissionGateV1 → actual system-role evidence policy + user evidence message → LFM2.5-2.6B QAD-Q4_0 / llama.cpp → BUFFERED raw generation → strip completed reasoning block → final-answer citation/format validation → answer or insufficient evidence.

GENERATOR POLICY
Product: exact LFM2.5-2.6B-QAD-Q4_0 artifact behind GeneratorBackend. Research/control: official MoR 360M plus Vanilla/Recursive baselines on desktop. SmolLM2 is historical only. MoR mobile deployment is optional research and cannot block the product.

TRAINING POLICY
No product-generator training is required. MoR evidence-use SFT is optional research only and must use separate held-out evaluation; no training activity may delay the product path.

CRITICAL PATH
KPack + hybrid retrieval + secure Android pack runtime + Granite query embedding + LFM2.5 product generator + buffered reasoning/citation validation + two signed demo packs + PackBench. No cloud inference, vector DB service, neural query rewrite, reranker, executable packs, or mandatory MoR Android runtime.

CAPSTONE PROOF
No pack → no pack-derived evidence. Install signed Repair pack → grounded cited procedural answer. Install bounded law pack → English grounded answer + exact source + historical as-of behavior. Corrupt pack → reject. Remove pack → pack-derived evidence becomes unavailable. These demos do not claim erasure of information from model weights.
